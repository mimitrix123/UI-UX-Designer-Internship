# User Research

## Research goal
Understand how people coordinate recurring care for themselves or a family member, where current tools fail, and what would make a care-management product feel trustworthy rather than overwhelming.

## Research method
A portfolio-ready mixed-method plan was used:
- 8 semi-structured interviews across self-managers, adult children, and partners
- Diary prompt covering one week of reminders, appointments, and shared tasks
- Competitive audit of general productivity, calendar, and care-management patterns
- Synthesis using affinity clustering

## Key themes
1. **Information is fragmented.** Tasks live across calendars, messaging apps, paper notes, and memory.
2. **Reminders need context.** A reminder without the person, reason, or next action creates friction.
3. **Shared responsibility is unclear.** Families need visible ownership so tasks are not duplicated or forgotten.
4. **Trust matters.** Users want transparent privacy controls and minimal collection of sensitive information.
5. **Busy moments demand speed.** Adding a medication, confirming an appointment, or marking a task complete should take seconds.
6. **Progress should reassure, not judge.** Users prefer simple completion trends over guilt-inducing streaks.

## Opportunity statement
Design a lightweight coordination layer that turns scattered care information into clear next actions, shared ownership, and calm visibility.

## Design principles
- Calm over clinical
- Action over information overload
- Shared by default, private by choice
- Explain before asking
- Accessible at a glance
