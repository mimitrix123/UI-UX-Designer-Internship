# Behance / Dribbble Case Study Layout

## Cover
**CareLoop**  
*A calmer way to coordinate everyday care.*

## Section 1 — Problem
Care tasks are scattered across calendars, chat threads, notes, and memory. CareLoop turns that fragmentation into one shared, actionable plan.

## Section 2 — Research
Show: research goal → interview themes → affinity clusters → opportunity statement.

## Section 3 — Personas
Show Priya and Arjun with goals, pain points, behaviors, and needs.

## Section 4 — Journey
Show the end-to-end journey and highlight the Today view as the highest-value moment.

## Section 5 — Competitive analysis
Use a compact matrix comparing scheduling, task ownership, recurring work, documents, family coordination, insights, and privacy.

## Section 6 — Information architecture
Show the navigation and object model: care member → tasks → medications → appointments → documents → activity.

## Section 7 — Wireframes
Present the 16 low-fidelity frame descriptions and the evolution toward the final visual system.

## Section 8 — Design system
Display color tokens, typography scale, spacing, radii, components, states, and accessibility rules.

## Section 9 — High fidelity
Feature the 16-screen board. Lead with Home, Today, Medication, Calendar, Shared Tasks, Documents, Insights, and Settings.

## Section 10 — Prototype
Show the three clickable flows with motion specs: medication completion, appointment confirmation, and shared-task assignment.

## Section 11 — Validation
Explain the five-user usability test plan and success metrics: task completion, time-on-task, navigation errors, and confidence.

## Section 12 — Outcome
CareLoop makes responsibility visible without overwhelming users. The product is intentionally a coordination layer, not a replacement for clinical systems.

## Closing
**Designing for care means designing for clarity, trust, and the next small action.**
