# CareLoop — Full UI/UX Case Study

## 01 — The challenge
Care coordination is often managed through a patchwork of calendars, chat threads, notes, reminders, and documents. The problem is not a lack of information; it is a lack of **shared context and ownership**.

## 02 — The opportunity
Create one calm place where a person can answer four questions instantly:
**What needs to happen? For whom? When? Who owns it?**

## 03 — Research
Interview synthesis surfaced fragmentation, reminder fatigue, ambiguous ownership, privacy concerns, and a need for fast actions.

## 04 — Personas
Priya represents self-management. Arjun represents family coordination. Their shared need is clarity without complexity.

## 05 — Competitive landscape
General calendars are strong at time. Task apps are strong at productivity. Health apps are strong at personal metrics. CareLoop connects those patterns around coordination.

## 06 — Design strategy
1. Today-first information architecture
2. Contextual reminders instead of generic alerts
3. Explicit ownership on shared tasks
4. Progressive disclosure for complex inputs
5. Privacy controls beside sharing actions

## 07 — Wireframes
Low-fidelity screens established hierarchy, one dominant action per screen, and a consistent bottom navigation model before visual styling.

## 08 — Design system
A calm blue/teal palette, Inter typography, generous spacing, rounded surfaces, accessible targets, and reusable states create consistency without feeling clinical.

## 09 — High fidelity
The final concept contains 16 screens spanning onboarding, home, care members, tasks, medications, appointments, shared coordination, documents, notifications, insights, and settings.

## 10 — Prototype
The key prototype proves the medication, appointment, and shared-task flows. Motion is purposeful and reduced-motion alternatives are defined.

## 11 — Validation plan
Test five representative users on three tasks:
- Add a recurring medication
- Confirm an appointment
- Assign a shared task

Measure task completion, time-on-task, navigation errors, and confidence rating. Target: ≥90% task completion without moderator intervention.

## 12 — Outcome
CareLoop turns a fragmented responsibility into a visible, actionable daily plan. The design is intentionally focused: it coordinates care without attempting to replace clinical systems.

## 13 — Reflection
The strongest design decision was making **Today** the product's center of gravity. Instead of exposing every feature immediately, the interface prioritizes the next useful action and lets users progressively explore detail.
